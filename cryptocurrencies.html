Major cryptocurrencies explained

A starting point for understanding the projects most often discussed in the market. These pages explain what each network is trying to do, where it can be useful, and what risks deserve attention. They are not investment recommendations.

₿

Bitcoin

BTC

The original cryptocurrency and the market's most watched store-of-value experiment. Bitcoin uses a public, proof-of-work network to let participants transfer value without a central operator.
Ξ

Ethereum

ETH

A programmable blockchain used for smart contracts, decentralized applications, stablecoins, and digital assets. ETH pays for activity on the network and helps secure it through staking.
≃

Solana

SOL

A high-throughput network designed around fast, low-cost transactions. Its ecosystem includes trading applications, payments experiments, games, collectibles, and other consumer-facing projects.
✕

XRP

XRP

The digital asset of the XRP Ledger, a public network focused on fast settlement and transfers. Its story is closely followed for payments, liquidity, legal, and regulatory developments.
◆

BNB

BNB

The token used across the BNB Chain ecosystem for fees and applications. It is associated with Binance, but the exchange, blockchain, and token should be evaluated as separate parts of the story.
$

Tether

USDT

A widely used dollar-pegged stablecoin that sits at the center of crypto trading and settlement. Its usefulness comes from liquidity and availability, while reserves, transparency, and counterparty risk deserve attention.
$

USD Coin

USDC

A dollar-pegged stablecoin used for transfers, trading, and applications across several networks. Users should understand the issuer, reserve disclosures, network fees, and the risks of holding a tokenized dollar.
A

Cardano

ADA

A proof-of-stake blockchain known for a research-led development style and a focus on formal methods. Its long-term case depends on real application use, developer activity, governance, and execution.
Ð

Dogecoin

DOGE

A payments-focused meme coin that grew from internet culture into a large, liquid market. Community attention is part of its identity, but price can be especially sensitive to sentiment and social-media momentum.
A

Avalanche

AVAX

A smart-contract platform built around a multi-chain architecture and applications. Its network activity, developer ecosystem, validator design, and token economics are more useful to study than a single price move.

How to use these pages

Start with the project overview, then compare the live chart and market data on the homepage. A large market cap does not automatically mean low risk, and a technically interesting network is not automatically a good investment. Read the risks and check primary sources before making decisions.
