Today's article

The market is calmer on the surface than it feels underneath. Bitcoin is holding near $65,000, ETF demand has returned for a few sessions, and traders are leaning harder on derivatives while they wait for a clearer macro and regulatory signal.

Friday, August 7, 2026 · Written for TopCryptosNews

Crypto is having one of those weeks when the headline price does not tell the whole story. Bitcoin is hovering around the mid-$60,000s and Ether is near $1,900, but the more revealing action is happening in the plumbing: how traders are using futures, where ETF money is going, and how quickly a regulatory story can change the mood.

Bitcoin's quiet range is hiding a louder derivatives market

Bitcoin has spent much of the past two months above $60,000 without finding a convincing break in either direction. That has left traders watching the same familiar levels while the activity around the market keeps changing.

Cointelegraph, citing CryptoQuant data, reported today that Binance's Bitcoin futures volume was nearly eight times its spot volume. The figures were striking: roughly $57.8 billion in daily futures volume against about $6.1 billion in spot volume. That does not tell us whether Bitcoin must rise or fall. It does tell us that leverage, hedging, and short-term positioning are doing more of the work than straightforward buying.

That distinction matters. A futures-heavy market can move quickly when positions are crowded, even if the underlying spot market is quiet. Bitfinex Research also described volume as thinning near the edges of the range, with options traders preparing for the possibility that September brings a downside resolution. For now, the cleaner reading is patience: the market is waiting for a reason to leave the range.

ETF flows have improved, but fear has not disappeared

U.S.-listed spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded $244.4 million of net inflows on Wednesday, according to SoSoValue data reported by Cointelegraph. That made three straight days of inflows and brought the short run total above $626 million. BlackRock's IBIT led the three-session period with about $479 million of inflows.

Ether funds also had a better day, taking in roughly $60.9 million for a second consecutive day of net inflows. XRP products moved the other way, with about $3.6 million in net outflows. The split is a useful reminder that “crypto ETF demand” is not one single trade. Bitcoin, Ether, and XRP funds can be responding to different investors and different stories.

The caution is that sentiment measures are still weak. The Crypto Fear & Greed Index was reported at 25, in Extreme Fear territory. Strong ETF days are encouraging, but a few sessions of inflows do not erase the broader hesitation. Traders will want to see whether the money keeps arriving when prices stop bouncing.

The Clarity Act is now a September story

The U.S. Senate will not vote on the crypto market-structure bill before its August break. CoinDesk reported that the Senate is expected to return on September 14, with a possible vote in the following weeks. The delay does not kill the bill, but it removes the near-term event that many in the industry had been preparing for.

The remaining arguments are not just technical. Ethics language, stablecoin yield and rewards, agriculture committee provisions, and law-enforcement concerns are still part of the negotiation. A bill that has made progress through committees can still become stuck when the final coalition has to agree on the details.

For the market, the important change is timing. Traders now have another month to debate what the legislation might mean for exchanges, token issuers, stablecoins, and the balance between the SEC and CFTC. That gives headlines more room to move sentiment, but it also means the next clean catalyst is farther away than many expected.

Tether is taking its tokenization push into Saudi real estate

Tether said it will provide the technology for tokenized institutional real-estate assets in Saudi Arabia through Hadron, its asset-tokenization platform. First Data will act as issuer and market operator, while BKN301 is expected to connect the platform to banking and compliance systems.

The announcement is less about a new trading token and more about the slow effort to put traditional assets on blockchain rails. The partners said the work could later extend to energy, infrastructure finance, and other real-world assets. Saudi Arabia's Vision 2030 program gives the project a broad modernization backdrop, but the practical test will be whether institutions actually use the system and whether the legal ownership structure is clear.

That is the part worth watching. Tokenization headlines often sound bigger than the first product. The real measure is whether settlement becomes easier, reporting becomes more transparent, and investors receive rights they can understand rather than simply a digital receipt.

What I am watching next

  • Whether Bitcoin can hold the mid-$60,000 area without needing a fresh futures squeeze.
  • Whether ETF inflows continue after the three-day streak, especially if price momentum fades.
  • Any Senate language that clarifies the September path for the Clarity Act.
  • Whether Ether continues to attract stronger fund flows than Bitcoin on individual sessions.
  • Evidence that tokenization projects are moving from announcements into live, regulated use.

The market does not look decisively bullish or decisively broken today. It looks selective and easily startled. Bitcoin is still the anchor, but the next meaningful move may be decided by leverage, rates, regulation, or fund flows rather than by a dramatic change in the technology itself.
